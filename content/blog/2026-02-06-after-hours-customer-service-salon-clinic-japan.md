## When Do Customers Actually Reach Out?

The mismatch between business hours and customer behavior is one of the most overlooked problems in the service industry. Consider these patterns:

**Evening browsers.** Working professionals often research salons and clinics after dinner — between 8pm and 11pm. They browse Instagram, visit websites, and send inquiries. If nobody responds until the next morning, many will have already booked elsewhere.

**Weekend planners.** Saturday and Sunday are prime times for people to plan their week ahead. A potential patient looking for a dental appointment or a client wanting to book a hair treatment sends a message on Sunday night — and waits until Monday for a reply.

**International time zones.** For salons and clinics in Tokyo that serve international clients, time zone differences create additional gaps. A potential client in New York researching Tokyo dentists is browsing at 10am EST — which is 11pm or midnight in Japan.

**Emergency inquiries.** Dental clinics in particular receive after-hours calls from patients with urgent concerns. While an AI can't provide medical advice, it can offer reassurance, share emergency protocols, and ensure the patient's information is captured for a callback first thing in the morning.

## The Real Cost of Being Unavailable

Most salon and clinic owners understand they miss some after-hours inquiries. Few realize the scale.

If your salon receives even five after-hours DMs or missed calls per week, that's roughly 20 per month and 240 per year. Even if only a third would have converted to bookings, that's 80 lost appointments annually — potentially millions of yen in lost revenue for a busy salon.

For dental clinics, where patient lifetime value is significantly higher, the impact is even more pronounced. A single new patient who can't get through after hours and books with a competitor instead represents not just one lost appointment, but years of ongoing care.
